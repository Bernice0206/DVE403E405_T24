Chapter 1 · National trend

Cases climbed for a decade, then April 2020 emptied the wards

Q1. How did non-fatal road traffic hospitalisations change between 2011 and 2021, and how did 2020–21 compare with the pre-pandemic peak?

Each row of the heatmap is a year and each cell is a month. Rows darken as cases rise, from 34,033 in 2011 to a peak of 39,866 in 2019. The palest cell in all eleven years is April 2020, when the first COVID-19 restrictions kept people off the roads. Cases were back to normal levels by the second half of the year.

Reporting breaks. Victoria stopped counting emergency-department-only cases from July 2012, and NSW made a similar change in June 2017. Rows marked with a break are not directly comparable with earlier years.

Chapter 3 · People and severity

The most common injuries are not the most serious

Q3. Which age groups and road users have the most cases, and which stay longest in hospital?

Each bubble is a group. Further right means more cases per year; higher up means longer stays, our measure of severity. Bubble area shows total bed days. Car drivers are the most common patients but have short stays. Pedestrians and people aged 75 and over are fewer, but they stay about twice as long as average.

Bed days per case use 2016–2021 only, because a rehabilitation coding change in 2015–16 raised recorded stays. Small groups near the axis are unlabelled: hover or Tab to them to read their values.

Chapter 4 · States and territories

Most states rose; the NSW fall is a counting change

Q4. How did the direction and size of change differ across states and territories, and how does each one’s road user mix differ?

Raw counts mostly reflect population, so the map compares each state with itself. An index of 100 equals that state’s cases in 2013, the first year after Victoria’s reporting change. Blue states rose and orange states fell. Select a state to see its trend and road user mix.

Counts, not rates. The index shows change within a state. It does not show which state is safer, because the data has no population figures.

Chapter 5 · First Nations people

Road crash injuries look different in remote Australia

Q5. How does the profile of First Nations people’s road crash hospitalisations differ from that of non-Indigenous people?

First Nations people’s road crash hospitalisations rose from 1,167 in 2011 to 2,220 in 2021. Because the two groups are very different in size, the chart compares percentages within each group. The longer the line between the dots, the bigger the difference.

About the data

What is counted

A case is an estimated hospital admission with an injury diagnosis (ICD-10-AM S00–T98) caused by a road vehicle traffic crash. Transfers between hospitals are removed. Cases that ended in death within 30 days are excluded, and emergency-department-only visits are not counted.

Known limitations

  • Reporting breaks: Victoria from July 2012, NSW from June 2017.
  • A 2015–16 rehabilitation coding change raised bed days, so stays are compared for 2016–2021 only.
  • Counts are not population rates.
  • Small cells are suppressed (“n.p.”) and kept as missing, never zero.
